Sum of Consecutive Squares

Closed-form derivation and induction notes

In the analysis of algorithm complexity (such as nested loops in matrix multiplication or bubble sort passes), we frequently evaluate the sum of the first n integers squared.

Theorem 3.2: Sum of First n Squares
∑i=1n i² = n(n + 1)(2n + 1) / 6

For all integers n ≥ 1. For example, when n = 3: 1² + 2² + 3² = 1 + 4 + 9 = 14. Formula: (3 × 4 × 7) / 6 = 84 / 6 = 14.

Derivation Outline

  1. Telescoping Identity: Expand (k + 1)³ - k³ = 3k² + 3k + 1.
  2. Sum both sides: Accumulate the sum from k = 1 to n.
  3. Telescope LHS: The left side collapses to (n + 1)³ - 1 = n³ + 3n² + 3n.
  4. Solve for ∑ k²: Group linear terms and divide by 6.
